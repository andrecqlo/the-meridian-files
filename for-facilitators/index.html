The Meridian Files — facilitator guide

An EDI escape room that doesn't feel like training

A stealth equality, diversity and inclusion exercise, built as a 15-minute investigation game for teams. Nobody is told what it's for until after they've solved it.

Most EDI training tells a team what to look for and then asks them to spot it in a case study. This does the opposite: a team spends fifteen minutes doing rigorous, evidence-based investigative work — checking who a sample represents, whose challenge was heard in a meeting, what an average figure hides, which claims in a report are actually assumptions — with no EDI framing at all. Only in the debrief are they told that what they've just done has a name, and credited for having done it without being told to.

It plays like a point-and-click investigation game, not a workshop: one desk, a UV torch, a locked laptop, a pinboard, a fifteen-minute countdown. A team of two to six works it together on one shared screen — a laptop on a projector, a meeting-room display, or a phone passed around.

Who this is for

L&D and EDI leads looking for a training format that doesn't open with the word "training"; team managers who want a sharper alternative to a slide deck for an away day or induction session; anyone who has sat through unconscious-bias training that didn't land and wants to see whether a different structure does.

What's built

Case 01 — "The Evidence" — is complete and ready to run: a senior analyst has resigned overnight over a consultation exercise the board is about to sign off, and the team has fifteen minutes to work out why before deciding what to recommend. Cases 02–04 are in development.

Running it well

  • The reveal works once per office. Run it with teams close together — ideally the same day — and ask people not to describe the ending afterwards.
  • Say it's an investigation game for teams. Don't say it's training, and don't mention EDI, inclusion, or bias in the invite.
  • One person drives the screen; everyone else reads and argues. Groups larger than about six work better split across two screens.
  • The full facilitator notes — session timing, learning objectives, solutions, and debrief guidance — are linked above.

Format at a glance

  • 15-minute countdown, roughly 2 minutes of untimed debrief after
  • 2–6 players, one shared screen
  • Browser-based, nothing to install, works on a phone, tablet, laptop or projector
